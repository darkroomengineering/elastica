darkroom.engineering Brainstorm — Round 01 July 2026

Elastica

One continuous, scroll-driven physical world — not five clickable experiments. Branding, motion principles, storyboard, and a bench of new examples.

01 — Thesis

Physics as a design material, not a simulation.

Matter.js wants to be correct. Elastica wants to be expressive. It's the same relationship Lenis has to native scroll: not more featureful — more felt. The pitch isn't "rigid body collisions", it's: anything on your page can become a body. Words, images, buttons, the footer. Collision as a layout tool.

Lenis made scroll a feeling
Elastica makes layout a feeling
Together scroll becomes a force

Tagline candidates: "The unserious physics engine." / "Physics for people who failed physics." / "Everything bounces." / "Wrong on purpose."

02 — Identity

The rubber instrument.

Darkroom's family voice is precise, mono-annotated, engineering-deadpan. Elastica is the sibling that misbehaves — same lab coat, but it's bouncing a ball against the wall. The identity system: a rigorous grid that things physically refuse to stay inside.

Motion principles (brand = behavior)
1. Nothing teleportsEvery state change is a physical event. No fades, no cuts — things are pushed, dropped, or squeezed into place.
2. Everything settles, nothing stopsResting bodies keep a faint breathing jitter. The page is never a screenshot of itself.
3. The cursor has massThe pointer is a body in the sim at all times — it displaces, attracts, shoves. On touch: your thumb flicks are impulses.
4. Deadpan chrome, unruly matterUI labels stay Swiss-still (mono, hairlines) while content misbehaves. The contrast is the joke.
03 — Scroll model

One sim. Scroll is two forces.

There is exactly one Elastica instance for the whole page. Bodies never unmount — they are recycled, re-shaped, re-massed as you descend. The Lenis-smoothed scroll feeds the sim on two channels:

Channel A — position → choreography Scroll progress is the score. It morphs container walls, flips gravity, changes population count, swaps DOM↔canvas mode. Deterministic: same scroll depth = same world state. This is what makes it a site and not a toy.
Channel B — velocity → impulse Lenis velocity injects vertical force into every non-static body each frame. Scroll hard and matter gets shoved; stop and it sloshes back. Chaotic garnish on top of the deterministic score. This is what makes it feel alive.
Key trick The viewport is the container and the page scrolls through it — but the walls of that container are animatable (via containerOffsets). Acts hand off by squeezing bodies out of one regime and releasing them into the next. Scroll feels seamless because matter is continuous even when the "section" changes completely.
