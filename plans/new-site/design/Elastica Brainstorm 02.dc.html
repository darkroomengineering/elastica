00 — Budget first

The 100-body law.

Rule: DOM Elastica caps at ~100 bodies; anything denser is Canvas Elastica. Corollaries that shape the whole design:

SectionModeBodiesNotes
1 · Hero lettersDOM·OBB8Trivial. Spend the budget on solver substeps for a fat, juicy bounce.
2 · Pills / followDOM10–16Real DOM (text must be selectable/readable). Follow springs are cheap.
3 · Flocking textDOM→CVS~60 → 1k+Words stay DOM (≤100). If we explode into particles, that's the canvas handoff moment.
4 · Progress indicatorDOM1–3Persistent across page — lives in the global sim, never unmounts.
5 · FooterCanvas2–5kThe showpiece. Full-viewport canvas, spatial hashing earns its keep.
Only one section simulates at a time. Off-screen sections pause (ref.pause()); bodies freeze in their settled pose. IntersectionObserver is the conductor.
DOM bodies move by transform only. No layout/paint properties in update loops. Text nodes pre-measured once; resize re-measures, never per frame.
Degrade by count, not by feature. Low-end devices get fewer footer particles (5k→800), never a static fallback. The behavior IS the brand.

The Drop & The Magnet

Fold opens empty — beat of silence — then the eight letters fall, small honest bounce (restitution ~0.4, one rebound, settle). The settle event unlocks scroll (Lenis stays frozen until then; ~1.2s worst case, skippable by any input). From then on letters are magnetic: the cursor attracts them softly within a radius; they drift toward it, clump, and spring home when you leave. First scroll shears the clump off-screen — the letters visibly resist leaving.

8 bodies · OBB · attraction force in update() · settle = velocity epsilon check Risk: scroll-lock must be interruptible. Any wheel/touch during the drop fast-forwards the settle — never fight the user.

The Escort (Follow, kept)

Feature pills enter as a tidy static grid — each one a claim (2D collisions, DOM or canvas, frame-rate independent…). At the half-fold mark they unpin one by one (staggered, heaviest first) and become followers: trailing the cursor as a loose swarm while you read. They collide with each other, so the swarm has body. At the section's bottom edge they hit an invisible ceiling and can't cross — you scroll on and they bunch up against the boundary, left behind like dogs at a fence.

The Murmur (Flocking, kept)

The one paragraph of real copy (what Elastica is, in ~60 words). Past the trigger mark, words peel off the text block in reading order — as if the paragraph is being read aloud and the spoken words fly away — and school together as a flock circling the remaining text. Flock target drifts with scroll; cursor is a predator that splits it. Scrolling back re-inserts words into their sentence, again in order. 60 words keeps it DOM-legal; every body is still selectable text.

The Clutch (new — full-viewport canvas finale)

Full viewport, ink field, thousands of canvas particles. A static inner boundary shaped like the wordmark (or a keyhole/arch — per your Savee ref) sits center: particles are attracted inward but can't enter, so they clutch around the negative space, packing its outline in a dense, breathing crust. The word is drawn entirely by what's excluded from it. Scroll velocity sloshes the crust; the cursor bores a temporary hole through it; clicking the shape emits a pulse that flings everything and lets it re-clutch — the goodbye moment. Links (github / darkroom) are small static bodies particles also crust around.

CanvasElastica · 2–5k circles · static SDF boundary + inward attraction · pulse impulse Prototype first: crust packing quality is the whole effect. If circles pack ugly, mix 2–3 radii.
02 — Physical patterns

Nothing teleports — so every web cliché gets re-derived.

Case study: the progress bar3 concepts
A — The Roller ← recommended A red ball rolls along a top track. Position = scroll progress, but it has mass: hard scrolls make it overshoot and rebound off the track ends; stopping lets it roll back to true. Progress you can feel lying to you, then correcting. 1 body, near-free.
B — The Hourglass A slim edge column where grains drain top-chamber → bottom as you descend. Scroll up and they pour back. Beautiful but 100+ grains wants canvas — a second canvas layer running the whole page. Costlier.
C — The Peg Run Roller variant: section boundaries are physical pegs on the track. The ball audibly clacks through each one — progress becomes a tactile map of the page's structure. Pegs double as click-to-navigate anchors (the ball rolls there, never jumps).
Buttons / CTAPress = physical compression (wdth 62 squash), release = spring overshoot. Copy-to-clipboard emits a radial shockwave that shoves nearby bodies.
Link hoverUnderline is a rope: hover makes it sag under the cursor's weight; leaving twangs it straight. One spring, zero bodies.
Section transitionsNo fades. Content of the next section is visible early, held behind a static ceiling; crossing the threshold releases it to fall/settle into place.
Anchor navigationDeep links don't cut — Lenis scrolls there fast while the sim visibly rushes toward that section's state. Arrival always has momentum + settle.
LoadingThe hero IS the loader: the empty-fold beat + letter drop absorbs asset loading time. No spinner exists anywhere.
Tooltips / labelsHover labels are balloons on a short tether — they bob, get dragged by scroll wind, and snap back. (Use sparingly: 2–3 per page.)
